Private by design

Why Private, Offline Tools Can Be Easier to Reach For

Updated 2026-11-03

Written by Back Guides Editorial Team

When a hard feeling arrives, even a small barrier can feel like too much: a login, a password, a noisy feed, or wondering where your information will go. A private tool that is already on your phone can make the first step simpler. It does not ask you to explain yourself before you begin.

Privacy is not about hiding from support. It can be about making one gentle option easier to open when you need it.

Back Guides · 4 minute read

Different people need different kinds of support. Sometimes the first useful thing is another person. Sometimes it is a quiet minute before you decide what to say or who to contact. A private, offline tool can offer that in-between space without asking you to create an account or turn a difficult moment into a task.

Privacy tools are not a substitute for urgent help.

This guide cannot assess symptoms or tell you whether you are having an emergency. If you think you may need urgent medical help, contact your local emergency service. If you are in immediate danger or at risk of harm, seek emergency or crisis support now.

Fewer steps can matter

When attention is scattered, a familiar screen can be easier to reach for than a new service. There is no need to choose a profile name, remember a password, or decide how much to share. Back opens directly to six gentle options: Breathe, Distract, Ground, Move, Listen, and Read. You can choose one, choose another, or close it.

What “private” means in Back

Back does not ask you to create an account. The iOS app does not send your information to Back App or collect it on a server. Its App Store privacy label is Data Not Collected. Preferences, a saved support contact, and the small things you choose to keep stay on your device. You can read the full Back privacy approach at any time.

Offline can make a tool more dependable

The six core tools work offline. Sounds and exercises are bundled on the phone, so you do not need a connection to start them. That can matter on a train, in a waiting room, late at night, or anywhere you would rather not rely on a signal before you take one small step.

You still choose when to reach out

Private does not mean isolated. If you save a trusted contact, Back can open your phone’s call or message screen when you choose. It does not call or send anything by itself. You can use a quiet tool first, contact someone first, or do neither for a moment while you work out what feels possible.

Keep the boundaries clear

An app can offer a pause, a sound, a prompt, or a small way to orient. It cannot know what you are experiencing or replace professional or emergency care. If anxiety, panic, or overwhelm keeps affecting daily life, consider talking with a qualified health professional. If something feels urgent or unsafe, seek urgent local help.
