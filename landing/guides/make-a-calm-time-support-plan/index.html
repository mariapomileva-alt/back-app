Preparation

Make a quiet support plan during a calmer moment.

Not a promise that a hard moment will be easy. Just a few choices you will not have to invent from scratch later.

Back Guides · 5 minute read

When you are calm enough to think ahead, it can be kind to leave something simple for your future self. A support plan is not homework, and it is not a test of whether you are prepared enough. It is a short reminder of a few things that might make the next difficult minute feel less lonely or less crowded.

A plan is not a substitute for urgent help.

This guide cannot assess symptoms or tell you whether a situation is an emergency. If you think you may need urgent medical help, contact your local emergency service. If you are in immediate danger or at risk of harm, seek emergency or crisis support now.

Keep only what feels genuinely useful

You do not need to cover every possibility. Begin with one item from each of these three places:

  • One small action: sit by an open window, put both feet on the floor, take a short walk, or open a familiar sound.
  • One person or support route: someone you can message, call, or sit beside — plus a second route if the first person is unavailable.
  • One sentence to remember: “I can choose one thing.” “I do not have to solve this all at once.” Or words that sound more like you.

That is enough for a first version. A plan that fits on one small note is often easier to return to than a long list.

Choose options, not instructions

Try writing “I could…” rather than “I must…”. What helps can change from one day to the next. Breathing may feel useful one time; another time you may prefer a sound, a distraction, a grounding prompt, a little movement, or reading a few familiar words. Leave yourself permission to switch.

Make contact easier before you need it

If there is someone you trust, you might agree on a very short message that means “I could use company.” You can also decide what kind of reply helps: a call, a simple check-in, practical help, or quiet presence. Back can keep one trusted contact on your device and open the phone’s call or message screen when you choose. It does not contact anyone by itself.

Put the plan somewhere ordinary

A note by the bed, a pinned message, or a saved card in an app can be easier to find than something made especially dramatic. The best place is simply the place you are likely to notice. If seeing it all the time feels like too much, put it away and choose a different cue.

Let the plan change with you

Cross out what no longer helps. Add a new option after a difficult day, or remove an option that now feels like pressure. If anxiety or panic is recurring, worsening, or getting in the way of daily life, consider talking with a qualified health professional. More support can sit alongside your own small plan.
