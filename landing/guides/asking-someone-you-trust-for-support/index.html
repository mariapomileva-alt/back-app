Connection

How to ask someone you trust for support in a difficult moment.

You do not have to explain the whole story to ask for company, a call, or a small practical favour.

Back Guides · 6 minute read

Asking for support can feel like a large task when you are already overwhelmed. You may worry about being a burden, not knowing what to say, or needing to make your feelings sound reasonable. You do not need a complete explanation to make contact.

If you need urgent help, use urgent help.

A trusted person can be an important source of support, but they are not a substitute for emergency or professional care. If you think you may need urgent medical help, contact your local emergency service. If you are at risk of harming yourself or someone else, seek immediate crisis or emergency support.

Make the ask specific and small

People often want to help but do not know what would be useful. A short, direct request gives them something they can say yes or no to. You can adapt one of these:

  • “Could you stay on the phone with me for ten minutes?”
  • “I am having a hard moment. Can you send me a simple message back?”
  • “Could you sit with me? We do not have to talk much.”
  • “Can you help me decide what my next small step is?”
  • “I do not need advice right now. I would just like company.”

Specific does not mean demanding. It makes the request easier to understand.

Choose the format that takes the least effort

A call is not always the best route. A message, voice note, location share, or asking someone to be in the same room can be easier. Use the option that feels most possible. Back can keep one trusted contact on the device and open the phone’s call or message screen when you choose; it does not send anything by itself.

Make a plan in a calmer moment

If you can, ask your person what kind of contact works for them and for you. For example: “If I send the word ‘pause,’ would you be able to call when you can?” A shared plan makes it less necessary to find the right words later.

Support is collaborative. Your person can have limits, and you can still deserve care. A clear fallback — another trusted person, professional support, or emergency help — protects both of you.

If they do not answer

A missed call is not proof that you should handle the moment alone. Try a second person, a different support route, or a local professional or emergency resource if you need it. The important part is having more than one option.
