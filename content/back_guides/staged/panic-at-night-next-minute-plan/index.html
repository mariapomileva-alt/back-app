Panic Attack at Night: What to Do in the Next Minute

You do not have to solve tomorrow from the dark. The next small choice is enough for now.

Back Guides · 5 minute read

If a panic attack wakes you at night, you do not have to solve tomorrow from the dark. Sit up if that helps, notice one quiet detail, and choose one low-stimulation next step. Save the larger questions for daylight, and treat new or severe symptoms as urgent.

Do not assume a new or severe symptom is panic.

This guide cannot assess symptoms or tell you whether you are having an emergency. If you think you may need urgent medical help, contact your local emergency service. If you are in immediate danger or at risk of harm, seek emergency or crisis support now.

Make the room a little less demanding

Choose the smallest change that reduces pressure. You might sit up with your back supported, loosen something uncomfortable, take a sip of water, or move to a quieter corner of the room. If darkness feels too intense, use a low, familiar light. If light feels like too much, keep it low. There is no correct setting.

Give attention one quiet place to land

You do not need to force your mind to be calm. Try noticing one neutral thing: the texture of the sheet, a sound outside, the weight of a blanket, or the feeling of your feet on the floor. If counting or breathing makes you more aware of discomfort, you can leave it. A different route is allowed.

Choose one low-stimulation tool

Some people prefer a steady sound; others prefer a few familiar words, a grounding prompt, or a simple distraction. Back’s Listen and Read tools can be available offline, and its other tools can be paused, skipped, or changed. You are not meant to finish a sequence. You are only looking for the next option that feels less difficult than the one before it.

Let someone know, if that helps

A short message can be enough: “I am having a hard night. Could you check in when you see this?” You do not have to wake someone unless that is part of a plan you have agreed together. If you do decide to call or message, Back can open your phone’s system screen for a trusted contact when you choose; it does not send anything on its own.

Save tomorrow’s questions for tomorrow

You might place a note by the bed for anything that feels urgent to remember: a question for a health professional, a task to revisit, or a person to contact in daylight. Writing down one line does not make it disappear. It can simply give your mind permission not to hold it alone right now.

When more support belongs in the plan

If difficult nights are recurring, worsening, or affecting how you manage during the day, consider talking with a qualified health professional. Reaching for more support is not a failure of self-help. It can be the next kind thing to do.
